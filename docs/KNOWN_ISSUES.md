# Known issues and limits

* **Three of the five planned story chapters exist** (Redbank, Harvest, Ashwood ship; "The Town Where Nothing Burns" and "The Last Bell of Greyhaven" are not built). Generated expeditions provide the variety instead.
* **Solvability of generated villages is evidence, not proof**: about 85% are won by at least one scripted planner and about half by two or more; the rest are not hidden but are unverified (docs/BALANCE_REPORT.md). Planners are deliberately simple; a human may do better.
* **Laws almost never collapse once active.** In 300+ bot runs laws wavered or collapsed essentially never; public failures now cost conviction (phase 10) but rarely enough to break a law. Whether that makes belief too sticky is a question for the fun review.
* **Difficulty is measured on bots, never on people** (phase 11, `docs/BALANCE_REPORT.md`). Redbank's best plan (staged proof at the bridge, then messengers) still wins 998 of 1 000 seeds; the same route is far harder without the demonstration (bridge-builder 81.5%, greedy 65%, a social-only plan 0-8%). That is an upper bound for people, not a promise, and the bridge route's real cost (food: it ends with exactly the 4 food the objective asks for) is easy to miss.
* **Generic strategy variety is thin in Ashwood and Harvest**: only fire-lighting plans win Ashwood and only the moonlight law wins Harvest for the generic bots. The grain caravan and the decoy fire are won by the scripted planners, so those routes exist but no generic policy finds them.
* **The tournament's decision-density and rescue figures are proxies** (value by exact preview; rescue = another bot taking over with 1-3 days left), not measures of how a person feels.
* Not implemented from the phase 10 brief: faction compromise pacts that tax another resource, and factions blocking access to landmarks.
* Browsers verified (Playwright builds on Linux): Chromium 153 (whole E2E suite), Firefox 155 and WebKit 26.6 plus an iPhone-14 emulation (smoke spec only). Real Safari and every real device are untested; a screen-reader pass is owed (`docs/ACCESSIBILITY.md`).
* **Not deployed:** no URL exists and no live smoke test has been run (`docs/RELEASE_CHECKLIST.md`).
* From the AI-driven exploratory pass (`docs/PLAYTHROUGH_NOTES.md`): dimmed opening-story cards and the first-day tip card covering the map were confusing. **Fixed in phase 15** (`docs/FUN_REVIEW.md` FR-02, FR-03); generated villages do carry their own hints (only the river village reuses Redbank's, and it has a bridge).
* Art and audio are procedural placeholders of decent quality but not final.
* Human playtests have not been run. No claim about fun beyond automated evidence is made.
* **The last days are decided** (`docs/FUN_REVIEW.md` FR-07). A lost Harvest run can still be saved until three days remain if the player finds the grain caravan (34% of lost runs, measured with the scripted planners; the generic bots cannot find it, which phase 11 had misreported as 0%); with two days left or one, no run in any chapter was saved. A won or lost run's last day has no decision that changes the verdict. After a defeat the debrief now names the last day a different plan could still have saved the village. A decision with an uncertain outcome for the last two days is the next design change.
* **Belief changes the world late** (FR-06): the first law wakes on day 4-5 of a 7-9 day game. Days 1-3 are numbers (made visible by the morning report, but not yet physical).
* **Variety limits** (FR-08): one Redbank route wins 998 of 1 000 seeds; verified drought villages are nearly interchangeable (99.5% of random pairs share a winning plan).
* **A save file lets a human or an agent peek at the night's dice** and take it back (FR-09). Not a balance threat (measured), and not guarded: there is no leaderboard.
* **A loss by hunger or livestock names nobody** in the debrief (nobody died) and no event is told at household scale (story-lover persona, `docs/FUN_REVIEW.md`).
* **MCP: local stdio only.** No remote (Streamable HTTP) mode, so no live session shared between the browser and an agent (co-op is a save-file hand-off). Tested with the official SDK client and Claude Code; not with Claude Desktop, ChatGPT or other clients (`docs/MCP.md`).
* **Natural-language beliefs (Inventor mode, `docs/NL_BELIEFS.md`).** The offline reader matches a vocabulary; it does not understand language. On the final untuned set of 36 sentences it read 14 of 18 natural sentences as intended and refused all 12 that should be refused; real players will phrase things it has not seen, and typos are refused on purpose. A two-part sentence can be understood in part (the unused words are listed, the status stays "understood"). Only seven subjects and 40 subject/effect/trigger combinations exist (few of them new in Harvest or Ashwood), two written beliefs per run, no editing afterwards. **No human has used the composer**; how hard it is for people to land a belief of their own is unmeasured (in a real agent session the agent's own belief did not become law). Inventor runs are unranked by design.
* **The optional online interpreter has never run.** `server/interpreter.ts` and `src/ui/interpreter.ts` are tested with a mocked provider only: no key was authorised, no live provider call was made, nothing is deployed, and no model's accuracy, latency or cost has been measured. Rate limits are per server instance. Bring-your-own-key is not implemented.
* **Two advertised effects had not been executed** until phase 17 found them (a bell-powered river belief that moved no water, and a "cold" cost that was never applied). Both are fixed with regression tests (`tests/unit/advertised-costs.test.ts`); no audit of every advertised cost in the authored chapters against the code was done; only the effects the composer's 32 combinations touch were exercised.
* **Written beliefs are balanced against the built-in routes by bots and a formula, not by people** (`docs/FINAL_ACCEPTANCE.md`). An exploit hunt over every offered belief and the strongest compound pairings found one that beat the best built-in route (a bell-and-rain belief written on day 1 won Redbank 40 times in 40 against 36 for the best built-in plan); the fixes are a strength cap for beliefs about the whole of something and an unlock-day rule for answers the chapter holds back, re-measured at 40 seeds per cell (nothing beats the built-in routes afterwards). Eleven of the 38 cells would still win outright if the village fully believed them and their physical source were present every night (as the built-in laws would); what stops them is the cost of getting the village to believe, measured with a law-pushing bot. A person who is better at spreading belief than the bot could do better than these figures; nobody has tried.
* **Phase 17 changed no existing rule**: every recorded state and event hash of the built-in chapters and of the 1 000 generated villages is identical before and after (the engine version is still 1.0.0; the new `COMPOSE` action simply does not exist in older engines, which reject a run that contains one). The catalog of verified villages was therefore not regenerated for phase 17 (re-verified over seeds 1 to 1000: the same 853 villages, the same winning planners).
* **No service worker, no offline install**: the game loads from the network on first visit and then needs no further requests, but it is not a PWA and does not work from a cold start with no connection.
* **The novice pass loses every chapter, and telling alone changes nothing the score can see** (`docs/FINAL_ACCEPTANCE.md`): a script that follows the on-screen prompts through the real UI (the coach on days 1 to 3, then telling the most influential people, and in one style also pressing the proof buttons the Places list offers) lost all 24 runs, and the tell-only style scored *exactly* what doing nothing scores in all three chapters (493, 680, 697). It is a floor, a script rather than a person, but it means a newcomer who does only the obvious thing gets no sign that their actions mattered; the debrief's "what would have changed it" is then the teacher. Whether a first-time human can win a chapter in one or two tries is the main thing no automated check can say.
